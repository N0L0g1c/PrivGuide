Choose your privacy path

Not everyone needs a Faraday bag. Pick a level that matches your risk — then level up when you're ready.

Level 1

Everyday privacy

For normal people who want less tracking, fewer leaks, and better defaults.

  • Password manager + unique passwords
  • Signal for private chats
  • Firefox + uBlock (PrivyDeck optional)
  • 2FA on email & banking
  • Review phone app permissions

Level 2 · Recommended

Solid setup

For freelancers, journalists-lite, and anyone who wants a real private stack.

  • Proton or Tuta email
  • Mullvad / Proton VPN on public Wi‑Fi
  • Encrypted cloud, CryptPad docs, or local backups
  • LibreWolf or Mullvad Browser + uBlock
  • Separate work / personal accounts

High threat

Real life

Personal privacy beyond apps

The best VPN can't fix oversharing, weak locks, or a camera pointed at your desk. Privacy is a lifestyle system.

01

Social & identity

  • Audit public profiles — strip DOB, home city, workplace, family photos.
  • Use different usernames per context; stop recycling one handle everywhere.
  • Turn off face recognition and contact syncing in social apps.
  • Think before you post location in real time (wait until you've left).
  • Create separate email aliases for shopping, newsletters, and banking.
02

Home & physical

  • Cover laptop cameras; prefer mics you can mute in hardware.
  • Lock screens aggressively; enable full-disk encryption everywhere.
  • Shred or destroy documents with ID numbers — don't just recycle.
  • Question smart speakers in private rooms; segment IoT on guest Wi‑Fi.
  • Know who has physical access to your devices when you travel.
03

Money & shopping

  • Use privacy-friendly payment options where legal and practical.
  • Avoid store loyalty cards tied to your real identity when possible.
  • Don't photograph credit cards or IDs into cloud camera rolls.
  • Opt out of data brokers (varies by country — do annual sweeps).
  • Keep financial 2FA on hardware keys or authenticator apps, never SMS alone.
04

Communication habits

  • Default to Signal for anything you wouldn't put on a postcard.
  • Disappearing messages for ephemeral plans; screenshots still exist.
  • Verify safety numbers for high-stakes contacts.
  • Don't discuss secrets on employer devices or workplace chat.
  • Assume group chats expand your threat surface with every new member.

Mobile

Phone privacy — Android & iOS

Your phone is a tracking supercomputer you carry everywhere. Lock it down by platform.

Android hardening checklist

  1. Prefer a modern Pixel + GrapheneOS if you can. Otherwise keep stock OS updated.
  2. Install apps from trusted sources — Play Store carefully, or F-Droid for FOSS.
  3. Permission diet: deny location, mic, camera, contacts unless required. Use only while using.
  4. Disable ad ID / ads personalization and reset advertising IDs.
  5. Turn off unnecessary sensors and connectivity: Bluetooth, NFC, precise location when idle.
  6. Use a DNS blocker (Private DNS / RethinkDNS / NextDNS) to cut tracker domains.
  7. Lock screen: strong PIN/passphrase, biometrics OK as convenience with PIN fallback.
  8. Backup plan: encrypted backups you control — not only Google One defaults.
  9. Work profile / secondary user to isolate sketchy apps from your main life.
  10. Avoid sideloading APKs from random sites — supply-chain malware is common.

iOS hardening checklist

  1. Update iOS promptly — Apple's sandbox is strong; unpatched is not.
  2. Enable Lockdown Mode if you face targeted attacks (extreme but effective).
  3. Privacy and Security: review every permission; reset tracking; disable unused Bluetooth sharing.
  4. Apple ID hygiene: strong unique password, hardware key or passkeys, Hide My Email for sign-ins.
  5. iCloud: know what's syncing. Advanced Data Protection strengthens E2EE for more categories.
  6. Limit Analytics and Improvements and personalized ads.
  7. Safari: enable Fraudulent Website Warning; consider Firefox Focus or Brave for extra blocking.
  8. App Tracking Transparency: deny by default. Still assume apps track within their walls.
  9. USB accessories / Stolen Device Protection for physical threat models.
  10. Avoid shady configuration profiles and enterprise MDMs you don't understand.

Quick iOS stack

  • PrivyDeck · Signal · Bitwarden
  • Ente Auth · Firefox / Brave
  • Proton Mail · Organic Maps
  • Mullvad / Proton VPN
  • Lockdown Mode when needed

iOS is closed-source — compensate with strong Apple ID security and minimal app surface.

Computers

Desktop & laptop privacy

Laptops hold your life's work. Encrypt the disk, minimize telemetry, and stop treating browsers like dumpsters.

🪟 Windows

  • Use a local account when possible; limit Microsoft account sync.
  • Turn off advertising ID, tailored experiences, and diagnostic Full telemetry.
  • Enable BitLocker (or device encryption) + strong login.
  • Replace Edge defaults: Firefox + uBlock Origin.
  • Uninstall bloat; disable startup apps you don't recognize.
  • Windows Sandbox / separate browser profile for risky sites.
  • Consider switching to Linux when you're ready for a cleaner slate.

 macOS

  • FileVault on. Firewall on. Automatic updates on.
  • Review Privacy and Security permissions per app regularly.
  • iCloud: enable Advanced Data Protection if suitable for your recovery setup.
  • Prefer App Store or verified developers; Gatekeeper stays on.
  • Limit Continuity features that broadcast presence if threat model requires it.
  • Use Firefox/Brave; containerize work vs personal browsing.
  • Lock screen hot corners + short idle timeout.

🐧 Linux

  • Full-disk encryption at install (LUKS).
  • Fedora, Debian, or openSUSE for sane defaults; avoid random gaming ISOs.
  • Flatpak + Flathub with care; prefer distro packages for system tools.
  • Firewall (firewalld/ufw); automatic security updates.
  • Qubes OS if you need strong app isolation (steeper learning curve).
  • Keep firmware updated (fwupd) — hardware matters too.
  • Separate browser for banking vs general use.

Universal desktop rules

Encrypt or lose

Lost laptop without FDE = full data breach. Always encrypt disks.

Password manager everywhere

Browser-built-in is better than nothing; Bitwarden/KeePassXC is better.

Updates are security

Unpatched OS/browser is the #1 easy compromise path.

Least privilege

Don't run daily work as admin/root. Don't install random .exe/.dmg.

Backup offline

3-2-1 backups with at least one offline/encrypted copy.

Public Wi‑Fi = VPN

Treat cafés and airports as hostile networks.

FAQ

Common questions

Do I need a VPN every day?

Not always. A VPN hides your traffic from local networks and your ISP, and can reduce location-based tracking — but it does not make you anonymous, and a bad VPN is worse than none. Use a reputable no-logs provider on hostile networks; combine with HTTPS, good browsers, and account hygiene.

Is Signal really private if it needs my phone number?

Signal encrypts message content excellently. Your number is an identifier for discovery — a tradeoff for usability. If that matters for your threat model, look at Session or similar. For most people, Signal is still the best everyone-will-actually-use-it choice.

What's the single highest-impact change?

A password manager + unique passwords + authenticator 2FA on email. Most account takeovers are reused passwords and SIM swaps, not nation-state zero-days.

Should I delete social media?

If you can, it helps a lot. If you can't, lock down profiles, strip personal data, stop real-time location posts, and use a browser container so trackers don't follow you across the whole web.

Is this site tracking me?

No analytics, no cookies for advertising, no third-party trackers for ads. Checklist progress stays in your browser's local storage only. Verify with your own blocker if you like — that's the point.

Where should I go deeper?

Read project docs for tools you adopt (Signal, Bitwarden, GrapheneOS, Mullvad). Community resources like privacyguides.org and official security whitepapers beat random top-10 VPN lists.
